## Paper 1
- Citation: S. Haque, M. Keffeler, and T. Atkison, "An evolutionary approach of attack graphs and attack trees: A survey of attack modeling," in Proc. Int. Conf. Security and Management (SAM'17), CSREA Press, 2017, pp. 224-229.
- What it does (1 line): A survey of attack modelling with attack graphs and attack trees, covering alert correlation, graph generation, tree reduction and Bayesian risk analysis, with the limits of each.
- Difference from AttackPath (1 line): It reviews methods and builds no tool; AttackPath ranks paths from a plain Nmap scan and is evaluated against ground truth.
- Points to use: nodes are states and edges are transitions; large graphs suffer state explosion; logic-based generation depends on admin-defined rules; one cited work uses CVSS in a Bayesian attack graph.

## Paper 2 (penetration path planning)
- Citation: Z. Chen, F. Kang, X. Xiong, and H. Shu, "A survey on penetration path planning in automated penetration testing," Appl. Sci., vol. 14, no. 18, Art. no. 8355, 2024, doi: 10.3390/app14188355.
- What it does (1 line): Surveys how attack paths are planned in automated penetration testing, grouping methods into intelligent planning, reinforcement learning and LLM-based planning.
- Difference from AttackPath (1 line): The surveyed methods plan or execute attacks using models or simulations; AttackPath only ranks and explains paths from a real Nmap scan.
- Points to use: tools such as Nmap still need experts to interpret results; attack graphs scale badly on large networks; the survey reports a view that CVSS-based attack graphs do not capture a network well; the gap between simulation and real networks is an open problem.
